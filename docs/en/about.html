pictkura  /  Why pictkura exists

A storehouse for
years of photos

pictkura did not start as an attempt to build a fast RAW viewer. It started with wanting one place where years of family photos stay openable, however many cameras came and went. This page is the reasoning behind how it ended up working. You do not need to read it to use the app — what it can and cannot do is in the specification.

1Somewhere to put them, and open them again

What I wanted was something like Sony's PlayMemories Home. Put the card in, the photos land on the computer, they line up by date, and something from years ago opens the same way as something from this morning. That is the whole of it.

Managing photos is not the hobby. Neither is developing RAW. But you still need somewhere to put what you shot, and a way to go back to it. There is no shortage of tools for taking pictures; the one for keeping them had quietly gone away. That is where this started.

2Photos outlast cameras

Shoot for long enough and the camera turns over before the photos do. A DSLR, a compact, a mirrorless body, then a phone. Each one brings its own format: JPEG, CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, RAF, DNG, HEIC, and video alongside them.

The equipment history is a patchwork, but the family's years are continuous. The folder from the year a child was born and the folder from this month should be one motion apart. A library that breaks along format lines cannot do that, which is why photos, RAW and video sit in the same grid here.

So format coverage is not a feature to boast about. It is the premise. Twenty-three RAW extensions are confirmed to give up a display image, checked against 28 real files from 16 manufacturers.

3The day JPEG was not enough

JPEG is enough almost always. You look at them the same evening and send a few to the family the same evening. Some days it is tempting to shoot nothing else.

But the days that matter most are the ones with the worst light. A wedding. Backlight. A room with a bright window in the frame. Night. When the highlights clip or the exposure goes wrong there, there is one frame you would like to rescue — and that thought almost never arrives on the day you took it.

4RAW as insurance, not as a workflow

Which leads to the principle this is built on.

Keep RAW as insurance, not as a workflow.

Day to day you should never have to think about RAW; it opens as fast as a JPEG. On the rare day one frame has to be saved, you take that one file to a real RAW developer. pictkura does not develop RAW because of how it wants them kept, not because the feature ran out of time.

What it actually does is simple: it pulls out the JPEG the camera already embedded for its own rear screen. Nothing is demosaiced, so a landscape frame — a full-size image out of a 24MP CR3 — takes 17ms, against 6ms for handing over a JPEG directly. Near enough the same. The colour is whatever the camera rendered. Portrait is the exception: the preview has to be rotated and re-encoded, which measured 218.5ms on a Nikon Df NEF (the numbers).

5pict + kura

pictkura is picture plus kura, the Japanese word for a storehouse.

A kura is not a workshop where things get made or finished. It is where something valuable is put away for a long time and taken out when it is needed. The thick walls and the small windows are not decoration; they are why what goes in is still there decades later.

A storehouse is not a darkroom.

That is the name, and it is also the test a feature has to pass. Not "would this be handy" but "does a storehouse need it". The reason the limitations are stated up front is not apology for what is missing — it is that the workshop was left out on purpose.

6Which is why it works this way

Most of the specification follows from the above. The technical choices did not come first with the idea bolted on afterwards; it went the other way.

BecauseIt works like this
This is not where photos get developedRAW is shown from the display JPEG the camera embedded. Nothing is demosaiced
What you put in comes back as you left itPhotos stay in their own folders. Nothing is imported into a private catalogue. Remove pictkura and the folders are untouched
What is in the storehouse stays in itLocal-first. No account, and the only outbound request is the update check. Files that exist only in the cloud are never fetched behind your back
Every year on the same shelfPhotos, RAW and video in one grid. A file from a ten-year-old camera opens the way this month's does
Taking something out should not mean waitingTens of thousands of files and the grid still moves. Speed is not the goal; it is the minimum a storehouse owes you
Destruction comes last✕ during culling moves nothing. It marks the frame, and when you close the viewer the marked ones are listed for confirmation and sent to the OS trash together

The last row is the odd one out. Speed goes into showing you things; anything irreversible is deliberately slowed down. It is the line that keeps you from throwing away what you put in the storehouse, and it is the one place nothing was made faster.
